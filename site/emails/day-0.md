Hey there!

You're in. Welcome to The MRI Educator's Blueprint.

For the next 5 days you'll get one email a day. Each one covers one mistake MRI educators make when they bridge classroom theory and clinical practice, what it costs your students, and a fix you can use in your next class.

One note before we start. This is educational content about how to teach and structure practice. It does not replace your program's safety training, your site's protocols, or your own judgment. Safety decisions stay with qualified staff.

And if you think a simulator can never feel like a real scanner, you're in the right place. I agree that no simulator replaces a real scanner. We'll cover what practice tools do well and what only clinical time can teach.

### Here's what's coming

**Mistake #1: Teaching Physics Equations First**
Students recite TR and TE on the exam, then can't use them at the console.

**Mistake #2: Leaving Hands-On Practice to Clinical Placements**
Students watch from the back of the scan room, and what they see depends on which site they landed at.

**Mistake #3: Assigning "Go Practice On Your Own"**
The login nobody opens after week two.

**Mistake #4: Grading the Final Picture Instead of the Choices Behind It**
A lucky image earns full marks, and you find out at the externship site.

**Mistake #5: Teaching One Scanner's Buttons Instead of the Principles**
The student who knows one console and freezes at the next.

### What you'll have at the end

A way to teach parameters that students can see, a practice plan that gives every student the same baseline before placement, and a grading method that shows you who understands and who got lucky.

Check your inbox tomorrow for Mistake #1.

Rodney
