### The Mistake: Leaving Hands-On Practice to Clinical Placements

Clinical placements give students real patients, real scanners and real pressure. They also give students a lot of time at the back of the room. A busy scanner runs on a schedule, and the tech at the console has a patient in the bore. Students end up watching more than they scan, and what they watch depends on the site. Two students in the same cohort can finish the same rotation with very different experience.

This mistake leads to the following:

- **Watching replaces doing:** A student can spend a full shift observing and never touch the console.

- **The cohort splits by site:** One site runs brain and spine all day. Another runs body and cardiac. Your students come back with different gaps.

- **Rare exams stay rare:** A student who never sees a given exam on placement meets it for the first time as a working tech.

- **Mistakes cost too much to make:** On a live patient schedule, a student can't try a setting just to see what happens.

### The Reason This Happens

Why does placement carry the whole load? It always has. Scanner time is expensive, patient care comes first, and programs assume students will log enough reps in the clinic. That assumption is the gap.

Here are some more specific reasons:

- **Tradition:** MRI education grew up as classroom first and apprenticeship second.

- **Scanner time is scarce:** Teaching scanners cost money and run clinical schedules.

- **Patients come first:** That's right, and it limits what a student can try.

- **Programs count hours, not reps:** Placement hours get tracked. Practice scans per student often don't.

### How To Fix It

Let's say the obvious part first: no simulator replaces a real scanner, and this email never says it does. A practice environment earns its place by doing one job. It lets a student change a setting, see the result, and try again with no patient waiting. Clinical time keeps the jobs only it can do: patients, coils, positioning, communication and the pace of a real list. Split the work that way and each side does what it does best.

Instead of leaving practice to placement, do this:

- **Set a baseline before the first rotation:** Write a list of practice scans every student completes before placement. Every student starts clinical with the same minimum.

- **Reserve placement for what only placement teaches:** Positioning, coil setup, patient communication and workflow. Tell students and sites that this is the focus.

- **Ask each site what students will and won't see:** Then fill the gaps with practice scans on the exams a site rarely runs.

- **Count reps, not just hours:** Track practice scans per student the way you track clinical hours.

Educators describe the gap the same way. Ayman Darwish, an MRI instructor at NAIT, says on Corsmed's site: "Before Corsmed, there was a big gap between didactic and clinical."
